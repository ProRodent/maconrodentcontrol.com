Macon Rodent Control is a locally-owned rat and mouse removal company headquartered at 3180 Vineville Ave in Macon, GA, serving Bibb County and the surrounding Middle Georgia region. The company was founded in 2023 with a narrow charter. Handle rodent work in Macon better than the regional and national operators do. The reason is simple. Macon's housing stock, climate, and tree canopy don't behave like Atlanta's or Savannah's. We don't do termites, mosquitoes, bedbugs, or general pest control. We do rats, mice, and the structural sealing that keeps them out, and we do that across Bibb County and the adjacent towns within about a 45-minute drive.

🔧 Technician insight

Most rodent control work in Macon is done with the same playbook used in Atlanta or Charlotte. That's the mistake. Macon's housing stock, climate, and tree canopy don't behave like Atlanta's. A 1920s Vineville Craftsman needs heritage-friendly materials and balloon-frame trap placement. A Cherry Street alley restaurant needs ongoing perimeter station work, not single-visit treatment. We built the business around those differences.

Why we started

The honest version: Macon homeowners and property managers were calling pest-control operators who treated rodent jobs as a sideline. A truck would show up, drop a few bait stations under the porch, charge a couple hundred dollars, and leave. The rats came back four months later. The mice never really left. Crawl spaces stayed open. Soffit gaps stayed open. The "treatment" was disposable.

The work that actually solves a Macon rodent problem is exclusion work. That means sealing every gap a rodent can fit through. The right material in the right place. After identifying the species and the entry route. That takes longer than a 30-minute drop-by. It also lasts. We built the company around doing that part right.

The canopy. Vineville, Ingleside, Shirley Hills, and the older sections of North Highlands sit under mature pecan and live-oak canopy that's been growing for a century. Every overhanging branch over a roofline is a roof rat highway. Treating a roof rat infestation in Vineville without addressing canopy access is a temporary fix.

The river. The Ocmulgee River corridor runs through East Macon, past Fort Hill, and along the eastern edge of downtown. Norway rats nest in the banks year-round. They push upland into homes during heavy rain. The pattern is predictable enough that we can tell which neighborhoods will get hit by which storm system.

The climate. Macon is humid subtropical. Mice breed year-round here. There's no winter die-off that resets the population for spring. The Cherry Blossom Festival in March overlaps with peak short-term-rental traffic, which overlaps with roof rats finishing their winter nesting cycle, and the calls stack up. We schedule around the local calendar because the local calendar is the rodent calendar.

What we believe about this work

Five operating principles, in plain language. Every job runs against these.

Identify before you treat

Norway rats, roof rats, and house mice each need different approaches. Treating without confirming the species is how problems come back. We confirm first.

Exclusion is the real work

Removing rodents is the easy half. Sealing every entry point so they can't come back is the half most operators skip. That's where the job actually gets earned.

Honest pricing, honest scope

Inspections are free. Quotes are itemized. If a job needs more work than initially quoted, we explain why before we proceed, not after.

Local depth matters

The treatment that works on a 1915 Vineville bungalow is not the treatment that works on a 2015 Wesleyan Woods build. We know the difference because we work both, weekly.

Follow-up is part of the job

Every exclusion job ends with a scheduled return visit to verify no return activity before we close. If something opened back up, we re-seal at no charge.

Pets and kids come first

Interior work defaults to snap traps and exclusion rather than rodenticide. When bait stations are used outdoors, they're tamper-resistant and placed where pets and children can't reach them.
