Napier Heights rodent profile

Napier Heights is an established Macon residential neighborhood with housing mostly from the post-war era, the 1940s through 1960s building that generates the consistent gap inventory described across North Highlands, Avondale, Kings Park, and Bellevue. Corroded foundation vents, mudsill settling on high-clay soil, unseal dryer vent gaps, and gas pipe penetrations combine with Macon's year-round subtropical mouse breeding to produce a persistent house mouse situation that is best addressed with systematic exclusion rather than ongoing trap-and-replenish programs. Norway rats are a secondary presence in Napier Heights' crawl space homes, and roof rats appear on blocks with meaningful canopy, though at lower density than in Vineville and Ingleside.

The most important thing we do on a Napier Heights inspection that a less thorough operator skips: we enter the crawl space. The mudsill gap, where the sill plate meets the foundation block, a joint that has been settling on Macon's clay soil for 60–80 years on most Napier Heights homes, is consistently the largest single rodent entry point on these properties, and it's invisible from the exterior. A Napier Heights homeowner who has had ongoing mouse problems despite multiple pest control visits has almost certainly had operators who didn't enter the crawl space and never found the mudsill gap. We find it, document it, and seal it properly on every engagement.
